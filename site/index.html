Send files from your Android phone to your Mac computer.

Tap Share in any app, pick your Mac, and the file lands in its Downloads folder. The two devices connect directly and encrypt everything end to end. There's no account and no cloud.

How it works

  1. Install both apps

    ATA on your phone (Google Play, coming soon), and the free ATA app on your Mac. It lives in the menu bar.

  2. Pair once

    Open ATA on the Mac and choose Pair New Device…. Then open ATA on your phone, tap Pair with a Mac, and scan the QR code.

  3. Share

    In any app, tap Share, then Send to Mac, then pick your Mac. The file goes to Downloads, or to a folder you choose.

What's different about it

No server in the middle

Files go straight from the phone to the Mac, over your Wi-Fi when you can, or over a direct peer-to-peer link when you can't. ATA has no relay server, so your files never pass through one.

Encrypted end to end

Pairing creates a key that lives only on your two devices. Every transfer is authenticated and encrypted with it.

Nothing to sign up for

No account, no cloud storage, no ads, no analytics. Neither app sends any data to the developer or to third parties.

Open source, both apps

The Android app, the Mac app, and the protocol specification are open source (MIT) on GitHub. You can read the code and check every claim on this page.

Honest limits

  • Nearby only. ATA finds your Mac over Bluetooth, so the phone and the Mac need to be close, about the same room. It can't send to a Mac somewhere else.
  • One direction. Android phone to Mac only. Not Mac to phone, and not from an iPhone or iPad.
  • Same Wi-Fi is the fast path. In our tests, about 60 MB arrived in around 6 seconds on the same Wi-Fi, including connection setup. When the devices are on different networks, the peer-to-peer path is slower (around 1 MB/s on mobile data in our tests) and uses your mobile data.
  • Most networks, not all. Some strict networks (some offices, schools, and public Wi-Fi) block direct connections. Because ATA never relays through a server, it then says that no direct path was found. Joining the same Wi-Fi fixes it.
  • The Mac app must be open (it stays in the menu bar after you close its window), with Bluetooth on. macOS 14 or later. Android 9 or later with Bluetooth LE.

Free

Both apps are completely free. There are no in-app purchases, no transfer limits, no ads, no subscription, and no account. Both apps are also open source (MIT).
