Help test ATA on Google Play

Before an app from a new developer account can be published on Google Play, Google requires a closed test: a group of testers who keep the app installed for at least 14 days. If you have an Android phone and a Mac, you can help.

Who can join

  • Anyone with an Android phone (Android 9 or later, with Bluetooth) and a Mac computer (macOS 14 or later) that you can use near each other.
  • You'll need the Google account you use for Google Play on that phone.
  • No technical skills needed. Using ATA the way you normally would is the most useful test.

How to join

Email magiclin99@gmail.com and include the Google account address you use on your phone (the one signed in to Google Play). That address is used only to add you to the tester list.

What happens next

  1. Once you're on the list, you get an opt-in link by email. Open it on your phone (or in a browser signed in to the same Google account) and choose to become a tester.
  2. Install ATA from the Google Play link on that page. It's free, like the public version will be.
  3. Install the free Mac app if you haven't yet (Mac setup), then pair your phone and send a few files.
  4. Keep ATA installed, and stay opted in, for at least 14 days. This is the part Google counts. You don't have to use it every day, but please don't uninstall it or leave the test early.

There's no fixed date for the public release. It depends on Google's review after the test.

Giving feedback

Anything that confuses you, breaks, or feels slow is worth reporting:

  • GitHub Issues, if you're comfortable posting publicly.
  • Email magiclin99@gmail.com, if you'd rather not.

The Help page lists what's useful to include. ATA sends no logs automatically, so your report is how a problem gets noticed.
