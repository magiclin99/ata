Frequently asked questions

What it does and doesn't do

Does it work with iPhone, iPad, or from the Mac to my phone?

No. ATA sends in one direction: from an Android phone (Android 9 or later, with Bluetooth LE) to a Mac computer (macOS 14 or later).

How close do the phone and the Mac need to be?

About the same room. ATA uses Bluetooth to find the Mac and start each transfer, so it can't reach a Mac that's out of Bluetooth range. There's no remote sending.

Do they need to be on the same Wi-Fi?

No, but it's much faster when they are. ATA tries the local network first. If the phone and the Mac aren't on the same network (for example, the phone is on mobile data), it tries a direct peer-to-peer connection over the internet. That path is slower, around 1 MB/s on mobile data in our tests, and it uses your mobile data.

How fast is it?

On the same Wi-Fi, about 60 MB took around 6 seconds in our tests, including connection setup. Your speed depends on your Wi-Fi. Across different networks, expect much slower speeds, roughly 1 MB/s on mobile data.

The app says "no direct path". What now?

Neither the local network nor a peer-to-peer connection could be set up. This happens on some strict networks (some offices, schools, hotels, and public Wi-Fi) that block direct connections between devices. ATA deliberately has no relay server to fall back on. Connect the phone to the same Wi-Fi as the Mac and try again.

What kinds of files can I send, and how big?

Any file you can share from an app, and several at once. ATA doesn't set a size limit. The Mac declines a transfer if its save folder doesn't have enough free space.

Where do the files go?

Into the Mac's Downloads folder by default. Change it in ATA on the Mac (Save to "…"…). If a file with the same name already exists, ATA adds a number instead of overwriting it.

What if a transfer is interrupted?

Files that finished arriving stay on the Mac. A file that was only partly received is deleted, and you can send it again. Each file is checked with a checksum before it's saved. There's no resume yet.

Can I pair more than one Mac, or more than one phone?

Yes. The phone shows every paired Mac and marks which ones are nearby, and you pick one each time. A Mac can be paired with several phones but receives from one at a time.

Does the ATA app need to stay open on the phone?

No. Once a transfer starts, it continues in the background, with progress and a cancel button in the notification shade. The Mac app must be running (it lives in the menu bar).

Privacy and security

Do my files go through a server?

No. On the same network, files go directly over your Wi-Fi. Otherwise they go over a direct peer-to-peer connection. ATA has no relay server and no cloud storage.

Then what are the "STUN servers"?

To set up a peer-to-peer connection across networks, each device asks a public STUN server what its own public IP address is. ATA uses Google's and Cloudflare's public STUN servers. They see the devices' IP addresses, which is unavoidable for any internet connection. They never see your files, file names, or anything else. Same-Wi-Fi transfers don't use them.

How is it encrypted?

Pairing (by QR code) creates a random 256-bit key shared only by your phone and your Mac. Every session is authenticated with it, and the connection details and files are encrypted (AES-256-GCM on the local network; DTLS on the peer-to-peer path, with fingerprints exchanged over the encrypted Bluetooth channel). The full protocol is published in the public repository.

Does ATA collect any data?

No. There are no accounts and no servers, and no ads, analytics, or crash-reporting SDKs. Neither app sends any data to the developer or to third parties. The only outside contact is the one in the STUN question above: when a peer-to-peer connection is needed, two public STUN servers see your IP address. Details are in the privacy policy.

Why does Android ask for "Nearby devices" (or Location on older Android)?

ATA needs Bluetooth to find your Mac. On Android 12 and later, that's the Nearby devices permission, which ATA declares as not used for location. On Android 9 to 11, Android itself requires the Location permission for any Bluetooth scanning. ATA doesn't read or use your location.

Why does ATA ask for the camera?

Only to scan the pairing QR code, and only when you tap scan. The code is decoded on the phone by an open-source decoder built into the app. Camera frames are never saved or sent anywhere. If you'd rather not allow the camera, choose Paste pairing link and paste the link shown on the Mac instead.

Is it open source?

Yes. The Android app, the Mac app, and the protocol specification (currently written in Traditional Chinese) are all open source under the MIT licence, in one repository: github.com/magiclin99/ata.

Can I verify the privacy claims?

Yes. Both apps are open source, so you can read exactly what they send and where: the Bluetooth handshake, the local-network transfer, the peer-to-peer setup with the two STUN servers, and the absence of any analytics or developer server. You can also build the apps yourself from the same repository.

Cost

Is it really free?

Yes. Both apps are completely free: no in-app purchases, no limit on transfers, no ads, no subscription. Nothing is locked behind a payment.

Compared with other options

Is this the same as AirDrop?

No. AirDrop is Apple's feature for Apple devices. ATA doesn't use or connect to AirDrop. It's a separate pair of apps that sends from an Android phone to a Mac.

Why not just use a cloud drive or a chat app?

Those work by uploading your file to a company's server and downloading it again, and they usually keep a copy there. ATA sends directly between your two devices and keeps no copy anywhere except the Mac you sent to. The trade-off is that ATA only works with the two devices near each other.

Mac app

Why does macOS warn me when I open the Mac app?

The Mac app isn't notarized by Apple (that requires a paid membership in Apple's developer program). Follow the Open Anyway steps once, or use the one-line install, which doesn't trigger the warning.

Transfers are slow, or ATA says "Local network connection was blocked"

ATA receives over your local network first. If the macOS firewall drops the phone's connection, the phone falls back to a much slower peer-to-peer path, and the Mac app shows Local network connection was blocked — check the macOS firewall. To fix it:

  1. Open System Settings → Network → Firewall → Options…
  2. Find ATA and make sure it's set to Allow incoming connections. If ATA is listed more than once (left over from an earlier version), remove the extra entries with − so only one ATA remains, set to Allow.
  3. Quit ATA (in the ATA window, Quit ATA) and open it again from Applications. The firewall may only apply the change after ATA restarts.

If macOS asks whether ATA may "accept incoming network connections", click Allow. You don't need to turn the firewall off.

Which Macs are supported?

Any Mac with Bluetooth running macOS 14 or later. The app is universal, so it runs natively on both Apple silicon and Intel Macs.
